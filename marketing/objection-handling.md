# Objection handling

Method: acknowledge, ask or answer with one fact, end with a question. Never knock a competitor. Never promise a feature that isn't live. No savings numbers. Price is {$299} CAD per month, **confirm before publishing**. Competitor facts below come from `sales/market-and-positioning.md` (dated 2026-11-07); competitors change prices and features, so **re-check any fact before saying it**. Where a fact is not there, I did not state one.

## 1. "It's too expensive" / "$299 is a lot"

**What it usually means:** value isn't proven yet, or they're comparing to a spreadsheet that costs nothing, or it's a negotiating move.

**Say:**
> Fair. Let's check it against your own numbers. How many hours a month does your team spend retyping estimates, chasing signatures, and sorting out job costs? If it saves less than the price, it isn't worth it. The trial is 14 days, no card, so you can test it on one real bid.

**Then:** offer the setup (rebuild one recent bid and compare totals). If they want a discount, only the founding offer applies ({40}% off for as long as they stay, first {25}, if enabled), because it's the same for everyone.

**Don't:** discount before they've tried it, or claim a payback period or a savings figure you can't back. Don't invent an ROI.

## 2. "Spreadsheets work fine"

**What it usually means:** it works because one person carries it in their head.

**Say:**
> Spreadsheets are free and everybody knows them, so I won't argue. Two questions: what happens to the bid sheet and the timecards when your estimator or office manager is away? And your last change order, did it get billed? If both are fine, keep the spreadsheet. If not, try one job in the trial and compare.

**Facts to use:** calculators drop priced lines in; regional prices and provincial labour with burden; the proposal comes from the same record as the estimate (no retyping); client e-signs from a link; job costing from approved timecards.

**Also offer:** "Keep your spreadsheet. Use the free calculators at {site_url}/tools alone, no signup."

**Don't:** tell them their way is wrong.

## 3. "We already use Jobber"

**Acknowledge:** Jobber is a well-known service-business app.
**Ask:** "How do you handle three things in it today: holdback on a progress draw, priced change orders the client signs, and LOTO permits?"
**Say:**
> Jobber is good for service calls. If most of your work is service and you want booking and dispatch, stay with it. If it's projects and shutdowns, with change orders, holdback and safety paperwork on the same job, that's where we fit. Do you bill in Canadian dollars?
**Fact (re-check before using):** `sales/market-and-positioning.md` says Jobber's pricing pages are in USD and per user or per tier.
**Don't:** say what Jobber can't do unless you've checked its current plan pages.

## 4. "We already use ServiceTitan"

**Say:**
> ServiceTitan is built to run a dispatch board for service calls. If most of your work is service with a call centre, it's likely the better fit. If it's projects, shutdowns and industrial work, we fit that work, and our price is one flat price for the company, not per technician. How many of your people answer calls versus work projects?
**Fact (re-check):** the sales notes say ServiceTitan is per-technician and not published.
**Don't:** quote a number for their price.

## 5. "We already use Procore"

**Say:**
> Procore is strong for general contractors. Many subs use it because the GC invites them. Is that your own licence or the GC's project? Some trade shops keep the GC's system for that job and run their own estimating, crew time, change orders and holdback in a separate tool. Where do your own estimates and timecards live today?
**Fact (re-check):** the sales notes say Procore's price is quoted by construction volume; ours is a flat price on the pricing page.
**Don't:** say TradeQuote PRO replaces Procore for a GC.

## 6. "We already use Simpro"

**Say:**
> Simpro is a deep system with real electrical depth and good progress claims. If you run a large service department plus projects and have the budget and time for an implementation, it may fit better. If you want something running this week, month to month, with a published CAD price, try ours on one job. What's your contract term with Simpro?
**Fact (re-check):** the sales notes say Simpro's price isn't published and it's sold per user.
**Don't:** repeat a reviewer's contract-term claim; it's one unverified review.

## 7. "Is my data safe? Where is it stored?"

**Say:**
> Each company's records are separated in the database, and each role sees only what it should, so an electrician sees their own timecards and not your margins. If a subscription lapses, you can still sign in and read everything. On where it's stored: {OWNER: confirm the database region and finish the Privacy Policy before answering; the current draft says data may be stored or processed outside Canada, including the United States}.
**Also:** point to the Privacy Policy once final. Ask: "What does your insurer or your client require?"
**Don't:** claim Canadian hosting, certifications, a penetration test, "bank-level" security or "unhackable". None are confirmed.

## 8. "Switching is a hassle" / "What about my existing data?"

**Say:**
> I get it, and I won't ask you to move everything. Run one job in parallel: bid it in TradeQuote PRO next to your usual way and compare. If you like it, move the next one. If you want, send me one recent bid and your client list, and I'll set it up and rebuild the bid so you can check the totals.
**Facts:** there is no bulk import promised here; saved assemblies and your price book are set up in the app. {OWNER: confirm whether a client list import exists before promising it; the setup offer says "we import your client list" and only holds if you do it by hand.}
**Also say plainly:** there's no live accounting sync; export a CSV for your bookkeeper; the trial is 14 days; cancel any time.
**Don't:** promise that migration is painless or quick.

## 9. "My guys won't use it"

**Say:**
> That's the right worry. The field screens work on a phone. Electricians only see their own timecards. For the trial, pick one foreman on one job and see if the daily log beats paper. Who's your most phone-friendly foreman?

## 10. "Can I trust the AI?"

**Say:**
> Treat it like a fast junior: it drafts, you review every line. The estimator never sends anything to a client by itself. The assistant shows its sources so you can check them in your code book. It summarizes; it doesn't replace the code or your judgement.
**Don't:** call it accurate, certified or compliant.

## 11. "You're new. What if you disappear?"

**Say:**
> Fair. It's month to month and you can cancel any time. If you stop, you can still read your records. {OWNER: confirm export options before promising any.} I'm {owner_name}, you can reach me directly at {support_email}. What would you need to see to feel safe trying it on one job?
**Don't:** invent customer counts, logos or "trusted by" lines. If there is real proof later, add it here with permission.

## 12. "Send me some info" / "I need to ask my partner"

**Say:** send the one-pager and the demo link, and ask one question: "Is your work mostly projects and industrial, or service?" Suggest they invite the partner into the trial as an admin from Settings, then Team.

## 13. "Maybe in the spring"

**Say:** "Makes sense. The trial clock starts when you sign up, so sign up when you're ready. Want me to check back in {month they name}?" Follow up only if they say yes, and note it.

## Rules

- A "no" is information. Ask why once, then stop.
- If they say "remove me", unsubscribe at once and send nothing.
- Log every objection and the outcome after the call. After 20 conversations, rewrite this file from the real ones.
