# 20-minute discovery and demo call

For a contractor who asked for it (a reply, a trial user, a referral). Do not cold call to book this. One person, one screen share, one job. Price is **confirm before publishing**. Run the call as a conversation, not a pitch.

**Before the call (5 minutes):** open their website; note their trade, size and the work they show; open `{site_url}/demo` in one tab and a prepared demo company in another; write down one question you want answered.

**If you will record,** say so and get a clear yes first.

## Agenda (20 minutes)

| Minutes | Part | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Open | Set the agenda, get permission |
| 2 to 8 | Discovery | Understand how they price and run jobs today |
| 8 to 16 | Demo | Show only what answers their pain |
| 16 to 19 | Next step | Agree one concrete action |
| 19 to 20 | Close | Recap, thank, send notes |

## 0 to 2: Open

> Thanks for making time. Here's what I'd suggest: about six minutes of me asking how you price and run jobs, eight minutes where I show you only the parts that fit, and then we decide if a trial makes sense. If it doesn't fit, I'll say so. Does that work?

## 2 to 8: Discovery (ask, then listen; take notes)

Ask these in order; skip any they've already answered:

1. "Walk me through the last job you bid, from the call to the proposal. Who did what?"
2. "Where does the estimate live? Spreadsheet, software, paper?"
3. "How do you work out labour and material prices? Where does a price come from?"
4. "How does a proposal get to the client and come back signed?"
5. "When the job is running, how do you know if it's making or losing money?"
6. "How do you handle extras and change orders? Last time one happened, how did it go?"
7. "Holdback and progress billing: how is that done today?"
8. "How does safety paperwork get done? LOTO, tailgates, daily logs?"
9. "Who else would touch this? Foremen, office, bookkeeper?"
10. "If this solved one thing, what would it be?"

Listen for: retyping, lost extras, labour guesses, missing paperwork. Write their words down; use them in the demo.

**Qualification check (in your head, don't read out):**

| Fit | Signs |
|---|---|
| Strong | Projects or shutdowns, 3 to 30 people, spreadsheet-based, has change orders and holdback |
| Weak | Mostly residential service calls with dispatch; needs live accounting sync; PDF takeoff on screen; bigger than the product suits |

If weak: say so kindly, offer the free calculators, and end the call early.

## 8 to 16: Demo (show only what they care about)

Open with their words: "You said {their pain}. Let me show that."

Pick **three** of these, in the order of their pain; don't show everything:

| If they said | Show (about 2 to 3 min each) |
|---|---|
| "I retype estimates into proposals" or "my labour numbers are guesses" | Choose a job, use a calculator (voltage drop or conductor sizing), watch the priced line drop into the estimate; show regional prices and provincial labour rates with burden, and where their price book overrides them; show markup and tax |
| "I want proposals signed faster" | The client portal: line items at sell price, accept and e-sign from a link, no account; the quote pipeline with follow-ups and win rate |
| "Extras get lost" | A change order with client signature |
| "I don't know if a job is making money" | Job costing from approved timecards; timecards with approval |
| "Holdback and invoicing" | Progress invoice with holdback and the GST/HST number; partial payments; the CSV for the bookkeeper |
| "Safety paperwork" | Daily log with photos on a phone; LOTO permit, tailgate meeting, safety inspection with sign-off |
| "Things lapse" | Alerts: overdue invoices, expiring insurance, WCB clearances |
| "I have a code question" | AI assistant: ask one real question; point out the sources; say it summarizes and they must check |
| "I'm short on time" | AI estimator: paste a short scope, show draft lines, then edit one line; say plainly it is a draft |

**Be honest in the demo:** say "this is reference pricing, replace with your supplier prices"; say the AI drafts and a human reviews; say it needs a data connection; if they ask for a feature we don't have, say so and note it.

Check in every few minutes: "Is this the kind of thing you'd use?"

## 16 to 19: Next step

> Based on what you told me, I'd try one real job in the trial: {their specific pain}. It's 14 days, no card. Want me to set up your company and rebuild a recent bid, so you can compare our total with yours?

Pick one next step and put a date on it:

- Set up their company with their client list and one recent bid (only if you'll do it).
- Invite one foreman to try the daily log.
- A check-in at trial day 5.

If they want to buy now: price is {$299} CAD per month for the whole company, with {the founding offer if enabled and spots remain}. Don't discount beyond the founding offer.

## 19 to 20: Close

> Thanks. I'll email you a summary and the link in the next hour. If you decide it's not a fit, tell me why. That helps me more than a polite no.

## After the call (5 minutes)

- Send a short summary: what they said, what you showed, the next step and date, links to `/demo` and `/tools`, the sender block. Their reply or booking counts as an inquiry; that's the basis for the email (existing inquiry).
- Log: pain, fit, objections, next step, date.
- Add objections you heard to `objection-handling.md`.

## Rules of the call

- Never promise a feature that isn't live.
- Never quote savings, speed or accuracy; let them judge on their own bid.
- Never badmouth a competitor.
- Don't use customer names or numbers.
- If they're on a competitor, ask what it does well first.
