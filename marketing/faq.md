## The product

**Who is it for?**
Canadian electrical and industrial contractors first, plus general trades. It suits shops with a few people up to a few dozen who bid projects, run change orders and bill progress with holdback.

**Who is it not for?**
Residential service shops that need dispatch boards, call booking and route planning; general contractors who need owner and architect collaboration on drawings; anyone who needs live accounting sync today or a full construction ERP; estimators who need on-screen takeoff from PDFs. ## Estimating

**What are the calculators?**
About 20: voltage drop, conductor sizing, conduit and box fill, motor circuits, CEC 8-200 dwelling load, EV charger circuits, transformer sizing, lighting layout, concrete, drywall, paint, framing, roofing, flooring, insulation, heat loss and water heater sizing. They drop priced lines into an estimate. Some are free to use on the public pages at /tools.

**Are the calculator results code-compliant?**
They are tools. Check results against the code edition and local rules that apply to your job. The calculators don't replace an engineer, an inspector or your judgement.

**Where do the prices come from?**
The regional cost engine has reference material prices and provincial labour rates with burden. They're a starting point, not live supplier quotes. Your company price book overrides them. Distributors often share pricing only per account, so enter your own prices where they matter.

**What does the AI estimator do?**
It turns a scope or drawing into draft priced lines. You review and edit every line before anything goes to a client. It can be wrong. Treat it like a fast junior who types quickly and needs checking.

**What does the AI assistant do?**
It answers Canadian code and construction questions and shows its sources, so you can check them. It summarizes and does not reproduce code text. {OWNER: confirm whether you hold a licence from CSA to quote code text before changing this.} It is not a code-compliance check.

**Does it handle lockout and orientation time?**
Yes: a non-productive time allowance as a percent of labour, and a provincial permit line.
